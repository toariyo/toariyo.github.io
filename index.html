Biography

I am a software engineer at Stripe, working within the Agentic Commerce Organization. I've also worked at several other companies like Mercor, Datadog, Microsoft, Amazon, etc. as a software engineer. Graduating from Princeton University, I earned an undergraduate degree in Computer Science. My research thesis, titled “Execution Trace Analysis for Understanding AI Agent Behavior—A Case Study,” focused on understanding how execution traces and log-based evaluation can reveal failure modes, reasoning patterns, and performance discrepancies in AI agents that are often hidden by aggregate benchmark metrics alone. This research was advised by Professor Arvind Narayanan, funded by Anthropic, and part of efforts led by the Science of Agent Evaluation Research Group. Moreover, I conducted independent research under Professor Olga Russakovsky, where I worked on detecting distracted driving using Computer-Vision Based Video Analysis. Apart from research, I served as the Director of Tigers in Product, Alumni Board of Directors for Princeton Entrepreneurship Club, and Vice Chair of the Engineering Council. My time at Princeton has been defined by a strong intersection of technical research, product thinking, and leadership, shaping my long-term interest in building impactful AI-driven systems and technology companies.

Borne out of a passion for problem solving, innovation within computer science has become my main area of intellectual curiosity. In high school, as one who always struggled to solve a rubix cube, I was fascinated when I learned how to use deep learning to teach a computer to solve a rubix cube during a summer internship at the Artificial Intelligence Institute within the University of South Carolina. Specifically, our team implemented a Deep Reinforcement Learning model (Planning Domain Definition Language). Afterwards, we applied Planning Domain Definition Language to assist in maximizing the probability of goal achievement, optimizing probability to 99% of all test configurations, finding a shortest path to the goal state 60.3% of the time and generalizing the model to other combinatorial puzzles. Furthermore, I was able to apply artificial intelligence algorithms to detect tumor cells in single-cell DNA sequencing data at the National Institutes of Health during a summer internship. What made these experiences particularly enriching for me was that it gave me a first glance at how powerful engineering can be as a tool for solving everyday problems. Engineering was no longer an abstract process that resulted in results only relevant to me in the context of my lab at school, but one that could help people across the world. These experiences bred my passion for computer science and guide me towards my future endeavors, where I aspire to utilize applications of computer science to assist in efforts to solve everyday problems in society.
