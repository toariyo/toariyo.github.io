Biography

I am a software engineer at Stripe, working within the Agentic Commerce Organization. I've also worked at several other companies like Mercor, Datadog, Microsoft, Amazon, etc. as a software engineer. Graduating from Princeton University, I earned an undergraduate degree in Computer Science. My research thesis, titled “Execution Trace Analysis for Understanding AI Agent Behavior—A Case Study,” focused on understanding how execution traces and log-based evaluation can reveal failure modes, reasoning patterns, and performance discrepancies in AI agents that are often hidden by aggregate benchmark metrics alone. This research was advised by Professor Arvind Narayanan, funded by Anthropic, and part of efforts led by the Science of Agent Evaluation Research Group. Moreover, I conducted independent research under Professor Olga Russakovsky, where I worked on detecting distracted driving using Computer-Vision Based Video Analysis. Apart from research, I served as the Director of Tigers in Product, Alumni Board of Directors for Princeton Entrepreneurship Club, and Vice Chair of the Engineering Council. My time at Princeton has been defined by a strong intersection of technical research, product thinking, and leadership, shaping my long-term interest in building impactful AI-driven systems and technology companies.

Across my prior experiences and my work at Stripe, I keep coming back to one question: how do you know an AI system actually works? At Amazon, on Supply Chain Optimization Technologies, I built networking systems that deliver products to more than 250 million customers with about 9% lower latency, which showed me how much small engineering decisions matter at scale. At Microsoft, I configured a Copilot-powered deflection bot that handles more than 80% of customer queries, my first time putting an LLM in front of real users. At Datadog, on AI Infrastructure, I built an LLM-powered system that maps more than 95% of cloud resource types to their Terraform equivalents, giving engineers a high-level view of their infrastructure. At Mercor, I worked on reinforcement learning environments and benchmarks, building evaluation harnesses that score model-generated code changes on grounded tool use, hallucination, and end-to-end task completion. Today at Stripe, I work on the Projects CLI, which developers and AI agents use to set up and manage the services their apps depend on. I led its migration to a single OAuth sign-in shared by agents and developers, and built a deploy gate for Stripe's Provisioning API: a production check that drives the released CLI end to end on every deploy, alongside scheduled evals that grade how agents get through onboarding. The common thread, from my thesis to today, is that aggregate metrics hide the failures that matter most, and the most useful engineering makes those failures visible and fixable.
